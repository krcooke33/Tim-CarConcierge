The elevator pitch — in Tim's voice

I'm an enthusiast doing this for other enthusiasts — with the connections, the craft, and the time.

I've been doing this informally for friends for years. I know the cars, I know the specialists, I know who to call and who to avoid. I'm on your side of the table, not across it — flat fee, no commission, no handoff. I'm one of you, just with the bandwidth to run the whole thing properly.

It's for people who know what they want (or are close to it) and would rather spend a Saturday driving than a Saturday refreshing Bring a Trailer. One person accountable — me — from the first call to the first drive. One fee, disclosed up front.

How it works — in Tim's voice

Five steps. I run them all.

  1. Intake. We'll sit down for 30 to 90 minutes so I can get the car right in my head — what you want, what you'd settle for, what's a deal-breaker, where the budget really sits. I write it up and send it back so we both know we heard the same thing.
  2. Curate. I come back with three to five real candidates, each with a point of view on the tradeoffs. Usually a week or two — longer if it's a rare one.
  3. Vet. I inspect it in person if it's within about 200 miles of Greenwich. Farther than that, I bring in a marque specialist I trust and vet it with them. I don't recommend a car I haven't laid hands on or had my people lay hands on.
  4. Negotiate. I handle the offer, the counter, the close. I charge you a flat fee, so I have zero incentive to push the price up. My job is to get you a fair number.
  5. Deliver. I coordinate enclosed transport end-to-end — photos at pickup, photos at delivery, paperwork filed. You get the keys and go drive it.
How it's priced — in Tim's voice

Flat fee for the whole arc. À la carte if you only want one step.

  • End-to-end. I charge a flat fee, tiered by where the car lands — roughly $2,500 for drivers under $50K, up to $7,500+ for collector-grade cars over $150K. That covers every step I just walked you through.
  • À la carte. If you only need one piece of the arc, I'll price it per scope — here's the menu so you can see what that looks like.
  • Transport. I bill it at carrier cost plus a small, disclosed margin. I won't hide it inside the fee.
  • Referrals, thanked. If someone sends you my way, I thank them — $500 flat, or a credit against a future engagement, their choice. I disclose it openly at intake. Reputation's the only real marketing in this work, and the people who share mine should be thanked for it.
  • Never. No percentage of purchase price. No kickback from the seller. No meter running on hours. You see the number before anything starts.

Two quick gut-checks — in Tim's voice

  1. Does this list make sense? Are any of these add-ons obvious "of course" items — things you'd expect me to handle? Are any of them stretches that don't really belong?
  2. What's missing? Is there a door you've wanted opened in the past that isn't on this list? Something you've gone looking for and couldn't find the right person for?
Key questions — in Tim's voice

Seven questions. Pick the ones that fit the person.

Stage direction, not a script: don't run these like a checklist. Two or three per conversation is plenty. The goal is honest feedback, not coverage.

  1. Does this problem ring true? When you last shopped for a car like this, where did it get painful — and who did you turn to?
  2. Would you have called me? If I'd been doing this back then, would you actually have picked up the phone? Honest answer — I'd rather hear no.
  3. Does the price feel fair? For the full arc — does $2.5K to $7.5K+ read to you as reasonable, cheap, or expensive for what I just described?
  4. What am I missing? What didn't I say that I should have? Is there a piece of this that doesn't exist yet but probably needs to?
  5. Who else should I be talking to? Who in your corner of the hobby would give me the sharpest read on this — whether they'd love it or push back hard?
  6. Would you use me? Not hypothetically — in the next 12 to 18 months, is there a car-search where this would've earned its fee?
  7. Does a referral thank-you land right? If I paid you $500 (or credited it against your next engagement) for sending someone who becomes a client — does that feel fair, too small, or off? I want the program to honor the reputation, not cheapen it.
What success looks like

Not consensus. Specific signals, after five to ten conversations.

A good outcome isn't everyone saying yes. It's a handful of sharp signals that tell us this is real — or that point us somewhere sharper.

  • Three people would call first. Unprompted, they say they'd use the service the next time they bought. Not "I could see it being useful" — actually "yes, me."
  • Two names offered without asking. Someone says "you should talk to [X]" before you ask for referrals. That's the surest sign the idea landed.
  • One objection we can't dismiss. A pushback sharp enough that it changes the service, the pricing, or the positioning. Sharpening beats validating.
  • New language in our words. We come out of the calls with a phrase or two we didn't walk in with — the way real clients describe the problem.
  • A specific car in play. At least one person says "I have a car in mind right now — can we talk?" That's not feedback. That's a first engagement.
Remember

The only wrong answer is a yes given out of politeness.

Tim, you'll want the kind feedback. Push past it. Ask one more question when the answer is soft. The sharp read is what tells us whether this is real — and the people on your list are the only ones who'll give it to you.
