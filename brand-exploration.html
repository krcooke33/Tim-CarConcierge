Directional decisions · 2026-04-25

Four directions taken — to steer the mood board.

An interview round closed four high-leverage choices. The remaining work moves from "explore everything" to "push these directions hard."

Ambigram
High conviction.

The 66 ↔ 99 idea is the heart of the brand — not just a mark. The era IS the brand. Every surface should let that idea breathe.

66/99 treatment

Of the 66/99 treatments, the slash leads as the dominant form. Sits below "The & Garage" in the hierarchy (see follow-up). The rotation glyph supports for stamps, favicons, hood badges.

Two tensions worth holding

High conviction + subtle departure. A central concept usually demands visual presence; a subtle departure asks for restraint. Resolution: the ambigram lives big on hero surfaces; everything else stays close to The & Network. Surface contrast does the heavy lifting.

Slash leads + ambigram is central. The slash is typographic; the ambigram is symbolic. The rotation glyph has to do meaning-making work even while the slash does brand work. Solution: rotation glyph appears next to or behind the slash wordmark — not as a sub-brand, but as visual proof of the wordmark's idea.

The lineage

Shared DNA, not a shared face.

& Garage should feel related to The & Network — clearly the same operator, the same instinct, the same character. But it shouldn't look like a copy. The visual identity needs its own texture, its own palette mood, its own object-world.

Tim's first business connects people to the right next chapter — talent to roles, leaders to networks. & Garage is the same instinct applied to a different object: connecting enthusiasts to the cars they've been chasing. The matchmaking shape carries over. The ampersand and the connector mindset carry over. The voice — direct, well-read, allergic to corporate phrasing — carries over.

What should diverge is the surface. The & Network is digital, professional, talent-flavored. & Garage is tactile, mechanical, era-flavored — workshops, dashboards, cracked leather, brushed aluminum. The visual system should announce that difference: a more analog texture, a different photographic vocabulary, a palette that nods to oil and copper and weathered enamel rather than corporate cream.

A note on this page

This exploration deck is styled in the & Network visual system on purpose — it sits inside Tim's existing site and needs to read as part of that family. The actual & Garage brand should depart from these surfaces. The mood board is where that departure starts.

Why 66 ↔ 99

Six and nine are rotational twins. The era is already a mark.

"1965 to 1999" isn't only a date range. It's a perfect ambigram — the same mark read two ways.

The numerals are doing the conceptual work for free: 6 and 9 are the same form rotated 180°, which means "66" and "99" can be the same mark seen from either end. That's not a clever device — it's the literal shape of the era we're claiming. The first analog-modern decade and the last one collapse into one glyph.

Open questions

What we still need to resolve.

A first round of brand exploration always surfaces more questions than it closes. These are the ones worth taking into the mood board.

Resolved
Mark

Primary mark or primary lockup?

#1 (rotation) is a true mark — quiet, unsigned, ownable. #5 (slash) is a wordmark. They could co-exist, but one has to lead.

→ #5 The slash leads. #1 supports.
Resolved
Ampersand

Inherit the & or redraw it?

The "&" is the family name. Two options: lift The & Network's exact ampersand glyph (instant recognition, risk of looking derivative) or draw a new one in a different face that holds the same role (more distinct, more work).

→ Inherit. Use the exact glyph for instant family recognition. The rest of the surface carries the distinction.
Resolved
Palette

Where does the palette depart?

Tim's existing system: ink dark + cream + lime accent + warm copper italic. The lime is the most recognizably-Tim color — keeping it would make the relationship obvious, dropping it would force real distinction.

→ Subtle departure. Keep warm copper, swap lime for a new signature (oxblood / brass / weathered green / off-white) — to be picked in mood board.
Resolved
Photography

Studio, drive, or detail?

Three different image directions: cars on lifts and stands (workshop), cars in motion on roads (drive), close crops of badges and dashboards (detail). The question was which mood is the emotional center.

→ Mix, context-led. Cohesion comes from a single grading direction (grain, contrast, color treatment) — to be defined in mood board.
Voice / byline

"by Tim Young" or just "& Garage" — and what's the tonal register?

Personal credibility is the moat (per the competitor scan), but "by Tim Young" can read as confidence or as caveat depending on placement. Voice tone — expert vs. enthusiast, formal vs. casual — is also unresolved. Mood board explores both in real surfaces (hero, business card, social header) so we can react in context.

Range

Does the mark scale down to favicon size?

Production test for #1: at 32px, do the rotated glyphs still read as 6 and 9, or do they collapse into noise? If they collapse, we need a simplified glyph for small surfaces.

What this exploration is for

A first cut. The mood board is where this gets material.

This is the strategic frame and the mark exploration. Next step: a mood board that turns the partial signals — palette, typography, motif, photography, voice — into a concrete-enough whole that we can react to it instead of arguing about it.
