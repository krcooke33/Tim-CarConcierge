01
Project & goals

What we're building, and what success looks like.

A focused 5-page site for The & Garage that launches as a validation tool for Tim's network and is built to extend into a lead-gen site as engagements compound.

What is The & Garage?

An acquisition concierge for enthusiasts buying classic and modern-classic cars from the era 1966 to 1999. End-to-end service, from the first conversation to the first drive — intake, search, vetting, negotiation, transport, handoff. Tim Young is the operator; the brand is a sibling to his existing recruiting practice, The & Network.

What is the site for?

Primary job · V1

Validation tool. A credible URL Tim sends to people in his network when he's setting up validation conversations. The site makes the conversation easier to have — it shows the offer, the operator, and the way of working before the call.

Secondary job · Phase 2

Lead-gen surface. Once Tim has 3 – 5 engagements behind him, the site becomes the front door for enthusiasts who don't know him yet. SEO-friendly, journal posts, real client quotes, gallery driven by Airtable.

Always

Tim's brand. The site is an extension of Tim's personal credibility. His name, his voice, his eye, his story. Everything else — palette, type, photography — supports that.

Success metrics for V1

The site doesn't need traffic to succeed. It needs to perform on three jobs:

  • Convert intent into conversation. When a network friend lands on the site, they finish on the Contact page or send Tim an email. Target: at least one out of three referred visitors completes an inquiry in the first 90 days.
  • Communicate Tim's specific competence. Visitors come away knowing Tim's era (1966 to 1999), his approach (end-to-end), and his eye (gallery + story). Tested informally: ask three friends what the brand does after they've spent two minutes on the site — answers should be consistent.
  • Hold up under enthusiast scrutiny. The site doesn't read as marketing; it reads as Tim. No lorem, no stock photo defaults, no generic "concierge" copy. If the BMW E30 forum lands here, they shouldn't roll their eyes.
Non-negotiable

Tim is the protagonist. The site is not a generic concierge service site with Tim's name on it. It's a site about Tim — a specific person with a specific eye and a specific era — that happens to offer a service. Every page should pass this test: could anyone else's name be substituted here without changing what's said? If yes, it's wrong.

Phase 2 · deferred

Search-engine optimization, journal posts, Instagram embed (replaces gallery), newsletter signup + email engine, Airtable-driven gallery feed. Build hooks for these in V1 (extensible structure, semantic HTML, named slots for journal & gallery feeds), do not implement.

02
Audience

Who lands here. What they need to feel. What they need to do.

Two cohorts at launch — Tim's network in V1, and self-found enthusiasts in Phase 2. Both are knowledgeable about cars; neither is a corporate buyer.

Cohort 1 · Tim's network (V1 primary)

Who

People Tim knows directly or one degree removed. Mostly enthusiasts in the Northeast US — collectors, weekend drivers, people with a dream car they've been quietly thinking about for years. Some have bought classics before; many haven't. They trust Tim or trust someone who does.

How they arrive

Three direct paths to the site, all skipping search:

  • Direct URL share — Tim sends a text, email, or message during a conversation. Most common.
  • Business card with QR code — Tim hands out at car shows, cruise-ins, and enthusiast events. Scan → site.
  • Flyer with QR code — posted at high-end independent mechanic shops in the Northeast (BMW, Porsche, vintage specialists). Scan → site.

Most visitors read on their phone first. Site must be phone-first. Each print channel uses a UTM-tagged URL (e.g., /?ref=card, /?ref=mechanic, /?ref=show-2026-04) so Tim can see channel attribution in Plausible — which materials are working, which aren't.

What they need to feel

"This is real." Tim's name and credibility transfer to the site. They should see Tim's photo, recognize his voice, and feel like they're in the right place. "This isn't a side hustle." The work is considered, the era is specific, the process is real. "I could send my dad here." No cringe, no generic concierge language, no asking for a credit card.

Implication for the site

The domain has to be print-friendly: short, memorable, easy to read at arm's length on a card. andgarage.com works; long alternatives don't. The QR code itself is a print asset (designed separately, not coded into the site). The site's only job is to handle the inbound visit cleanly — read the UTM, log the channel, present the same experience whether arrival was direct, scanned, or bookmarked.

What they need to do

Read enough to feel ready, then start a conversation. The conversion event isn't a purchase — it's a reply. They submit the intake form (Airtable) or email Tim directly.

Cohort 2 · Self-found enthusiasts (Phase 2 primary)

Who

Enthusiasts who find the site through search, social, BaT-adjacent communities, or a referral from outside Tim's direct network. They don't know Tim yet. They're more skeptical, more comparative, more in research mode.

How they arrive

Search ("classic car concierge northeast," "1966 mustang acquisition help"), referrals from forums, eventually social. They land on Home, About, Gallery, or a journal post — not a single guaranteed entry point.

What they need to feel

Same three feelings as Cohort 1, plus one more: "This person knows more than I do." Tim's gallery, Tim's story, Tim's process all work harder for this cohort because they're scrutinizing for expertise, not just trusting a friend's referral.

What they need to do

Read the site fully (5 – 10 minutes), check the gallery, read Tim's story, then start a conversation. Higher consideration than Cohort 1; the path through the site matters more.

What this means for V1

Optimize for Cohort 1 (network-referred). That means: trust Tim's name to do half the work, keep the page count small, prioritize the Home and About Tim pages, make the Contact path obvious. Phase 2 will revisit Home and add SEO infrastructure for Cohort 2.

Who this site is not for

  • Investment-grade collectors. The audience that wants a Pebble Beach-quality 250 GTO with provenance authentication and a wealth-management voice. That's Sublimity Lifestyle's market, not ours.
  • Buyers shopping on price. The site doesn't compete on lowest commission or fastest turnaround. If price is the question, Tim isn't the answer.
  • Modern car buyers. No Teslas, no 2010s Porsches, no exotic supercars from the last decade. The era is 1966 to 1999, full stop. The site should communicate this clearly enough that off-era inquiries self-deselect before contacting Tim.
03
Voice & tone

How the site sounds.

Two registers running together. The romance of the drive, and the white-glove handling that gets you there. Both, always, integrated. Equal weight (50/50).

The romance register

Sun-faded, bygone, free. The voice of someone who loves these cars, knows what they ask of you, and remembers when driving was the point. Underneath: a quiet awareness that human driving is becoming optional. Aware-not-preachy. Worn-warm, not bitter.

Pull-quotes for headlines, body, conversation

  • "For the dream car you've been chasing."
  • "While we still drive."
  • "Before they drive themselves."
  • "The last analog road."
  • "For the drive, not the destination."
  • "Some cars remember when driving was the point."

The handling register

White-glove, paired, accountable. The voice of a recruiter-turned-concierge — someone whose job is connecting the right person to the right thing. Tim is one of you who happens to do this for a living. Expertise is generous, care is total, language stays human.

Pull-quotes for headlines, body, conversation

  • "Hand-paired, enthusiast to enthusiast."
  • "From the first conversation to the first drive."
  • "Tim's eye on every step."
  • "White-glove, end-to-end."
  • "Vetted in person, delivered with care."
  • "One concierge, one car, one accountable hand."

Voice mechanics — do / don't

Do

First-person plural ("we'll find it") for the service. First-person singular ("I drive a 1968 Mustang") for Tim's own voice. Specific years and models — never "vintage cars" or "classic vehicles." The em-dash is the punctuation of the brand; use generously for tonal shifts.

Don't

No corporate-luxury vocabulary ("bespoke," "curated," "exquisite," "discerning clientele"). No exclamation marks. No emojis. No "we're passionate about..." opener. No "stewardship," "preservation," "keeper" — the brand is about driving, not museum-keeping. Avoid the word "vintage" — say "1966 to 1999" or "the era" or name the year.

Tim's first-person voice

The About Tim page and personal-signature blocks use Tim's actual voice — direct, dry, low-status, occasionally funny. His own line, "Yes, I eat my own cooking," is exactly the register. Do not over-write Tim. If a sentence sounds like a brochure, cut it.

For the build

When Replit's agent fills body copy, instruct it explicitly: "Use the voice notes above. Do not improvise. If a slot needs copy not provided, leave a placeholder labeled [NEEDS COPY] rather than inventing." This prevents the agent from drifting toward generic concierge prose.

04
Visual identity, locked

The brand system as code-ready tokens.

Locked decisions only. No optionality. Every token below is final and lives in CSS variables; every component lives as reusable markup.

Color tokens

Six core tokens. Light surfaces get oxblood as the accent; dark surfaces get copper. No invented variants. The retired --oxblood-bright token is gone.

:root {
  --ink-dark:    #131426;  /* primary dark surface */
  --cream:       #f7f3ec;  /* primary light surface */
  --offwhite:    #f3ede0;  /* warmer paper tone */
  --warm:        #c57b3c;  /* copper — accent on dark surfaces */
  --oxblood:     #6e2828;  /* accent on light surfaces */
  --oxblood-tint: rgba(110, 40, 40, 0.10); /* oxblood at 10% for backgrounds */
  /* Text */
  --text:        #1c1d34;  /* body text on light */
  --text-soft:   #4a4b6a;  /* secondary text on light */
  --text-dim:    #8a8ba3;  /* tertiary text on light */
  --text-on-dark:      #f7f3ec;  /* body on dark */
  --text-on-dark-soft: #c9cadc;  /* secondary on dark */
  --rule:       rgba(28,29,52,0.12);  /* light dividers */
  --rule-dark:  rgba(247,243,236,0.16); /* dark dividers */
}
Highlight color rule

Light surface (cream / offwhite / paper) → accent is oxblood. Dark surface (ink-dark) → accent is copper (warm). No other colors play the highlight role. This rule governs italic emphasis, the brand &, the year-mark connector, button hovers, and rules.

Typography

Two faces, both Google Fonts:

  • Fraunces — serif. Used for h1 / h2 / h3, hero, lede italics, brand wordmark, year-mark numerals. Weights: 300 (display), 400 (body), 500 (occasionally).
  • DM Sans — sans. Used for body text, kickers, captions, button labels, microcopy. Weights: 400, 500, 600.

Italic vs roman

Italic Fraunces is reserved for emotional emphasis in headlines and lede paragraphs. Roman Fraunces is for everything else, including the brand wordmark "The & Garage" — never italic. The italic Fraunces ampersand glyph (cursive Et-ligature) is intentionally avoided.

Year-range convention

Always use the word "to" — never em-dash, hyphen, or slash. Full form: "1966 to 1999". Compact: "66 to 99". Sub-eras: "1966 to 1974" not "1966 — 1974". One rule, no exceptions.

Photography treatment

Sun-faded, warm grain, golden hour. Portra 400 / Ektar 100 film feel. Categories: Drive (in motion, golden hour) · Arrival (cars at rest in atmospheric places, often with people) · Detail (wood wheels, gauges, gear knobs, leather close crops). Workshop / craft category is dropped from V1.

Grade recipe

  • Visible film grain (texture, not noise — light hand)
  • Soft contrast (don't crush blacks; preserve highlight detail in skies)
  • Warm color temperature (+amber, -blue) — late afternoon, never noon
  • Gentle desaturation (10 – 15%) — faded, not muddy
  • Lifted blacks, slightly milky shadows — Polaroid-faded rather than digital-clean
  • Reds and oranges hold their saturation; everything else relaxes around them

Motif: constellation dots

The dark hero across all pages carries a low-opacity constellation of dots (inherited from The & Network's visual system). Implementation as background-image with radial-gradient dots; no special component needed.

Brand non-negotiables

(1) "The & Garage" wordmark always uses .brand-amp. (2) Year ranges always use "to" — never dashes. (3) Italic is for emotional emphasis only — never for the brand name. (4) No invented colors outside the six tokens. (5) Photography uses the grade recipe — no slick-digital catalogue shots. Replit agent: enforce these as hard rules.

06.2 · About Tim

Purpose

Tim, in his own voice. The page where personal credibility — the moat — gets fully expressed. Visitors finish this page knowing Tim well enough to send him an email.

Sections

1. Hero — kicker "Meet Tim", h1 "I've been drawing cars since I was 5." Lead photo: Tim with a car, ideally one of his own (1968 Mustang, '93 BMW E30 316i Touring, or '97 4Runner — the cars he names in his story).

2. The story — Tim's full About story (his version 2, lightly tightened by ~15% for pacing). Real copy follows below.

3. Currently driving — short visual section: three cards, one per current car (Mustang · BMW · 4Runner), each with photo, year/make/model, and one line in Tim's voice. Optional but high-value — proves "I eat my own cooking."

4. CTA — "Let's find yours." button → /contact/. Tim's actual closing line from the story.

The story (real copy)

I've been drawing cars since I was 5 years old. That's when it started — the obsession, the love, the need to be around them. I grew up in a family of car enthusiasts and old-school NASCAR fans. Weekends were spent in the garage with my dad, hands covered in grease, learning how things worked. My first car was a 1973 Dodge Charger SE — my uncle's company car, passed down to me.

Over the years, I've owned numerous classics from that golden era between 1966 and 1999 — a 1985 Dodge Daytona Turbo Z, a 1967 Sunbeam Alpine, a 1985 Ford F-150, a 1987 BMW E30, a 1994 Toyota Land Cruiser, and more. Some were perfect. Some needed work. And one taught me everything I know about commitment.

My grandfather owned a 1974 Bronco Explorer, and I always wanted one of my own. I found a 1971 with a 302 and "3 on the tree" for $1,500 in Waynesville, NC. The motor wasn't as solid as the seller claimed. I could have walked away. Instead, I tore it down to the frame and rebuilt it from scratch. That full off-frame restoration became one of the projects I'm most proud of — not because it was easy, but because it reminded me why I love this. The hunt. The work. The moment you turn the key and it all comes together.

Today I drive a 1968 Mustang coupe, a rare 1993 BMW E30 316i Touring, and a 1997 Toyota 4Runner. I found all three for a combined $23k. Yes, I eat my own cooking.

Few people know I spent two years as Art Director at Lowe's Corporate, working with Team Lowe's Racing — Jimmie Johnson's #48 car and team #5 drivers Blake Feese and Boston Reid, part of Ricky Hendrick's driver development program. Or that I'm an illustrator. Most of what I draw? Classic cars.

For 13 years, I've been recruiting globally for creative agencies and consultancies. The & Network is my most recent venture. My job has always been the same: connect people to what makes them come alive. I've placed hundreds of professionals in roles where they thrive. Now I'm doing the same thing with cars.

If you're chasing a dream car from that era, I'll find it. Not because it's a service I offer — because I know what it feels like to want one. And I know what it feels like to finally get behind the wheel. Let's find yours.

Acceptance

Story renders as long-form readable typography (max-width 66ch). Tim's photo loads above the fold. CTA button visible at end of story without forcing scroll-to-bottom.

Photography & assets

Where the images come from. How they're treated.

Three categories. One unifying grade. Predictable filenames so the site can be coded and image management stays sane.

Categories

  • Drive — cars in motion, golden hour, open road. Cars as protagonists. Used in: hero images, gallery teasers, story moments.
  • Arrival — cars at rest in atmospheric places. Often with people. Used in: feel-section moments, About-page imagery, lifestyle shots.
  • Detail — close crops of wood wheels, gauges, gear knobs, leather. Cars as objects of close attention. Used in: process page steps, accent moments, alongside body copy.

Grade recipe

Apply uniformly to all photography. Tim or whoever is shooting can use Lightroom presets approximating:

  • Visible film grain (Portra 400 / Ektar 100 feel — light hand, not dirty)
  • Soft contrast — preserve highlight detail in skies, don't crush blacks
  • Warm color temperature: +200 to +400K white balance shift
  • Gentle desaturation: −10 to −15% global
  • Lifted blacks (~10% lift) for slightly milky shadows — Polaroid-faded, not digital-clean
  • Reds and oranges hold saturation; greens and blues relax

Asset folder structure

│   ├── home.jpg              (full-bleed home hero — required)
│   └── about.jpg             (about hero — Tim's portrait, required)
├── tim/

V1 hero strategy: only Home and About use full-bleed image heroes. Process, Gallery, and Contact use typographic heroes — kicker + h1 + lede on the dark ink-dark surface with the constellation motif, no full-bleed photo. Process content (the arc) is the visual; Gallery content (27 cards) is the visual; Contact's focus is the form.

Filename convention

  • All lowercase, hyphens between words.
  • Dates: YYYY-MM (gallery scouting items only — establishes recency).
  • Cars: year-make-model (e.g., 1968-mustang-coupe.jpg).
  • No spaces, no underscores, no capitals.

Alt-text rules

Every image gets descriptive alt text. Format: [year] [make] [model] in [color], [context]. Example: "1972 BMW 2002 tii in fjord blue, parked outside a brick warehouse". For Tim's portrait: "Tim Young, founder of The & Garage, leaning against a 1968 Mustang". Hero images get short emotional alt: "A 1968 Mustang on a Vermont back road, golden hour".

Image performance

  • Compress before commit (TinyPNG or ImageOptim) — keeps repo light, deploy fast.
  • Width: ~1600 — 2000px on the long edge for hero/gallery; ~800px for cards.
  • Format: .jpg for photography, .svg for any line art.
  • Always include width and height attributes on <img> tags to prevent CLS (cumulative layout shift).
  • Use loading="lazy" on images below the fold.

Channel attribution via UTM tags

Tim's print channels (business cards at car shows, flyers at mechanics) need to be measurable. Each printed QR code encodes a UTM-tagged URL so Plausible can attribute the visit:

https://andgarage.com/?ref=card                  /* business card */
https://andgarage.com/?ref=mechanic              /* mechanic flyer (generic) */
https://andgarage.com/?ref=mechanic-{shop-slug}  /* per shop, optional */

No code changes needed in the site — Plausible auto-captures URL parameters and exposes them as a "Sources" filter. Tim sees which materials are driving visits and inquiries. Phase 2 may add dedicated landing pages (/show/, /mechanic/) if a particular channel earns the investment.

Form integration · Airtable

Setup steps (Tim handles, ~15 minutes):

  1. Create an Airtable workspace (free).
  2. Create a base called "& Garage Leads" with a "Submissions" table.
  3. Fields: Name, Email, Phone, Chasing (long text), Timing (single-select: Now / This year / Still dreaming), Notes (long text), Submitted at (created time, auto), Status (single-select: New / Replied / Met / Closed).
  4. Open Form view → Share form → Embed → copy iframe code.
  5. Customize form: hide branding (paid feature, optional), match colors (background #f3ede0, button #6e2828).
  6. Set up Airtable Automations: when new submission lands, email Tim — automation built into free tier.
  7. Paste iframe into Contact page.

  • Tim's photography supply. If Tim doesn't have enough original photos for hero/gallery at launch, V1 ships with stock standins or limited gallery. Mitigation: commit to 5 core hero shots and 6 — 8 gallery cars before launch.

Never (or not unless the business shape changes)

  • Payment processing on the site. Engagement fees are invoiced and paid offline. No Stripe checkout, no PayPal button, no commerce.
  • E-commerce / "shop" / inventory. The gallery is scouting, not a storefront. Cars don't have prices on the site, can't be added to a cart.
  • Live chat / chatbot. Conversations happen via the form or email, person-to-person, on Tim's schedule. No automated response. The brand is human-paced, not always-on.
  • Comments / reviews / forum. Not a community platform. Validation happens via referrals and conversations, not on-site reviews.
  • Multi-language / localization. English only. Northeast US first, English-speaking market.
  • A heavy CMS (WordPress, Webflow CMS, etc.). Static HTML is the spec. Tim's blog (Phase 2) can use a static-site generator or markdown if needed; not a database-backed CMS.
  • Custom backend / Node server / database. Airtable is the data layer. No server-side code in V1 or Phase 2 unless a real need surfaces.
